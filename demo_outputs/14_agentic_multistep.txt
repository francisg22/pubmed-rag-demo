When comparing metformin and SGLT2 inhibitors for patients with type 2 diabetes
and chronic kidney disease (CKD), there are significant insights from the
literature.

1. **Efficacy and Safety Outcomes**: SGLT2 inhibitors have shown promise in
improving both cardiovascular and renal outcomes in patients with type 2
diabetes and CKD. In a systematic review, SGLT2 inhibitors demonstrated a
reduction in the composite risk of cardiovascular death, nonfatal myocardial
infarction, or nonfatal stroke by 19% and a decrease in heart failure by 39%
among this population. Additionally, there is evidence indicating SGLT2
inhibitors can slow the progression of kidney disease; they reduced the annual

renal outcomes by 29% [PMID 30697905]. As for safety, SGLT2 inhibitors did not
present additional risks specific to CKD patients compared to the general
population, although individual agents may vary in specific safety outcomes.

2. **Role of Metformin**: Metformin has long been considered a first-line
treatment for type 2 diabetes due to its efficacy in glycemic control and
potential cardiovascular benefits. However, its role in patients with advanced
CKD (specifically those with eGFR below 30 mL/min/1.73 m²) is limited due to
concerns about lactic acidosis. It remains generally effective in more mild
cases of CKD and even in patients with eGFR levels below 60 mL/min, where it has
shown protective benefits regarding kidney disease progression [PMID 40022102].
However, recent guidelines suggest that SGLT2 inhibitors provide superior
cardiovascular and renal protection when compared to metformin, particularly in
patients at high risk of cardiovascular events [PMID 40022102].

3. **Combination Therapy**: The combination of metformin with SGLT2 inhibitors
can be beneficial. While metformin alone may provide glycemic control and
potential renal protection, combining it with SGLT2 inhibitors might enhance
both glycemic and renal protective effects [PMID 40022102]. However, it is still
uncertain whether metformin offers additional kidney protection beyond what is
provided by SGLT2 inhibitors.

In conclusion, while both metformin and SGLT2 inhibitors offer benefits in
managing type 2 diabetes and CKD, SGLT2 inhibitors are increasingly recognized
as essential in this patient cohort for their significant cardiovascular and
renal protective effects. Therefore, clinicians might consider focusing on SGLT2
inhibitors alongside metformin, particularly in patients with CKD who are at
increased risk of cardiovascular complications.
