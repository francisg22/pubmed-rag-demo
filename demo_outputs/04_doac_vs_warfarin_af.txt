Direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs) are generally considered to have comparable
or superior efficacy and safety profiles compared to warfarin for stroke
prevention in atrial fibrillation.

1. Efficacy: "DOACs were at least as good as, if not superior to, warfarin in
terms of efficacy for the prevention of stroke in patients with non-valvular AF"
[PMID 27896543].

2. Safety: In terms of major bleeding incidents, "the direct oral anticoagulants
were associated with a similar or lower incidence of major bleeding compared
with warfarin" [PMID 26089678]. Specifically, under an uninterrupted
anticoagulant strategy, DOACs showed "significantly fewer major bleeding events
(2.0% versus 3.3%; RR, 0.62; 95% CI, 0.47-0.82)" compared to warfarin [PMID
29794081].

3. Convenience: DOACs offer several practical advantages, including "a faster
onset of action (time to peak concentration: 0.5–4 hours versus 36–72 hours)"
and "considerably fewer food and drug interactions" [PMID 26089678].

4. Risk of specific complications: "The risk of intracranial hemorrhage (ICH)
was 33%–70% lower in patients treated with a direct oral anticoagulant than in
those treated with warfarin" [PMID 26089678].

5. Patient adherence: Due to the lack of requirement for routine monitoring,
"DOACs can be administered at fixed doses without a need for routine coagulation
monitoring," which may improve patient adherence compared to warfarin, which
requires regular INR checks and adjustments [PMID 27924633].

Thus, DOACs are now often recommended as the preferred option for patients with
nonvalvular atrial fibrillation, providing a balance of efficacy, safety, and
convenience over warfarin.
