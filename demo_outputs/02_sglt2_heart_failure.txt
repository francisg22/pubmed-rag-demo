Yes, SGLT2 inhibitors reduce heart failure hospitalizations. A comprehensive meta-analysis that included five randomized controlled trials found that SGLT2 inhibitors significantly reduced the risk of hospitalization for heart failure, with a hazard ratio of 0.72 (95% CI 0.67-0.78) when comparing the SGLT2 inhibitor group to placebo. It was noted that treatment effects on hospitalization for heart failure were consistent across multiple trials involving a range of patients, including those with varying ejection fractions. The primary finding highlighted that "SGLT2 inhibitors reduced composite cardiovascular death or first hospitalisation for heart failure" [PMID 36041474]. 

Additionally, another meta-analysis noted that SGLT2 inhibitors were associated with lower rates of heart failure hospitalization, specifically highlighting that "SGLT2 inhibitors reduce cardiovascular events and renal outcomes in patients with diabetes mellitus" [PMID 33620621].
