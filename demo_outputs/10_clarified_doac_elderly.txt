  In adults aged 75 and older with atrial fibrillation, how do direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs) compare with warfarin for stroke prevention and bleeding risk?

GROUNDED ANSWER:

In adults aged 75 and older with atrial fibrillation, direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs) are considered at least as effective as warfarin for stroke prevention, with a lower risk of certain bleeding events. Specifically, "DOACs are at least as effective as warfarin for stroke prevention in elderly patients with NVAF. Compared with warfarin, DOACs were associated with reduced risk of intracranial hemorrhage" [PMID 28805310]. Furthermore, a meta-analysis showed that, in an uninterrupted anticoagulant strategy, DOACs are associated with significantly fewer major bleeding events compared to warfarin, "2.0% versus 3.3%; RR, 0.62; 95% CI, 0.47-0.82" [PMID 29794081].

Additionally, a systematic review indicated that among DOACs, apixaban has the lowest risk of non-major bleeding, suggesting it may be a safer choice within this group of medications [PMID 37310479].
