Statins may be considered for primary prevention of cardiovascular disease (CVD)
in adults over 75 years, but the evidence is mixed and recommendations vary.

1. Several studies suggest that statins can reduce the risk of major vascular
events in older adults. For instance, it has been reported that "for every 1
mmol/L reduction in LDL-C cholesterol in older adults (>75 years old), there is
a 26% proportional reduction in major vascular events" [PMID 36598445].

2. A recent study found that initiating statin therapy in adults aged 75 to 84
years resulted in a risk reduction for overall CVD incidence of "5-year
standardized risk reduction, 1.20% [95% CI, 0.57% to 1.82%]" [PMID 38801776].

3. Despite potential benefits, the U.S. Preventive Services Task Force concludes
there is "insufficient evidence to assess the balance of benefits and harms of
initiating a statin for the primary prevention of CVD events and mortality in
adults 76 years or older" [PMID 35997723].

4. The consensus suggests that statins may still be offered to older adults
following a shared decision-making process while considering individual factors
such as "life expectancy, polypharmacy, frailty, and potential adverse effects"
[PMID 33427435].

In summary, while there are potential benefits, particularly in those 75 to 84
years, the hesitance around initiating statins in those over 75 reflects a need
for more tailored approaches and careful consideration of risks versus benefits.
