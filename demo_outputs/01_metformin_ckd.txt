Metformin can be used in patients with moderate chronic kidney disease (CKD), specifically those with an estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) above 30 mL/min. Current guidelines have relaxed the previous strict contraindications, allowing for cautious use of metformin in such patients. 

Studies indicate that the risk of lactic acidosis (LA), which was a major concern, is overstated, as "the fear is disproportionate" with an incidence in non-CKD patients of about 6/100,000 patient-years, and this risk is not increased in metformin-treated patients [PMID 24711640]. Moreover, appropriate dosing adjustments based on renal function can further enhance safety. According to pharmacokinetic studies, adjusting metformin dosages according to CKD stages has shown that "there is not a relationship between metformin levels and plasma lactate" as long as dosages are appropriately managed [PMID 30061044].

Additional evidence suggests that metformin may provide cardiovascular benefits and is associated with reductions in mortality, even in the presence of renal impairment [PMID 27690331]. Hence, it appears that "many nations have already adopted this as a recommendation" for its safe use with an eGFR as low as 30 mL/min [PMID 28616207].
